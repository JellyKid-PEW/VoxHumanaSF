# Wild Huntress — Propulsion / Heat-Rejection Study

Status: **WORKING ENGINEERING STUDY — NOT LOCKED CANON**

This study follows the vertical-stack and mass/tankage work. Its purpose is to determine what the Huntress's aft machinery and exterior thermal hardware need to do before an exterior silhouette is designed.

## Realism baseline

In vacuum, normal spacecraft cannot remove sustained waste heat by convection. Heat must ultimately be radiated from external surfaces. Internal loops, heat pipes, pumped coolant, and heat exchangers move heat to those radiating surfaces.

For the Huntress, this means propulsion, power, electronics, habitation, galley, and ordinary machinery can be mechanically believable only if the ship has a visible thermal architecture.

The Huntress should therefore have:
- internal thermal collection / transport loops;
- body-mounted radiator area for ordinary cruise / habitation loads;
- higher-temperature aft radiator hardware for power / drive waste heat;
- thermal storage for short peak loads;
- an emergency consumptive heat-dump mode.

Magic is not used to erase this requirement. The crew's privacy practices favor conventional systems.

## Propulsion architecture — recommended working model

Do not treat the walk-in engine bay as "the engine."

The current Huntress should be understood as having a **distributed propulsion plant**:

1. **Compact energy-feedstock magazine**
   - small, protected, aft, inboard;
   - feeds the modern fusion-class energy source / driver;
   - exact fuel chemistry remains open.

2. **Energy source / power conversion**
   - deep aft and structurally tied to the ship;
   - provides electrical / field power to propulsion and ship systems;
   - may use direct conversion or another advanced method so only a fraction of drive energy becomes shipboard waste heat.

3. **Reaction-mass tanks and conditioning**
   - split across several aft-inboard cells;
   - feed pumps / conditioning / injectors accessible from aft service geography;
   - water can remain an emergency alternate reaction mass if later retained.

4. **Magnetic / plasma acceleration and nozzle structure**
   - aligned approximately through the loaded ship's center-of-mass corridor;
   - extends aft beyond the currently modeled occupied rooms;
   - may have several field / nozzle units rather than one giant bell;
   - thrust-vectoring and trim authority compensate for normal cargo-state changes.

5. **Walk-in engine bay**
   - a frequent-service room giving access to cooling manifolds, power-conditioning hardware, injector / field-support equipment, local bypasses, and diagnostics;
   - not the total drive volume;
   - this preserves the prose in which Iri can be waist-deep in a cooling manifold while the rest of the propulsion plant occupies inaccessible or deeper ship structure.

6. **Lower engineering / aft-service access**
   - reaches pumps, trunks, reaction-mass conditioning, field hardware, and deeper crawl/service zones;
   - connects into the same propulsion network from a different maintenance level.

This supports the locked principle that maintenance is integrated throughout the ship.

## Thrust axis and the offset bridge

The bridge does not need to sit on the thrust axis.

The important alignment is:
- **effective thrust line**
- through or close to the **loaded center of mass**
- with enough vectoring / trim authority to accommodate changing cargo and fluid states.

The bridge can remain offset if the hull and internal mass layout make sense.

Working exterior implication:
- the main drive / nozzle cluster should be visually centered on the ship's mass-bearing aft body rather than centered under the bridge viewport.

## Thermal architecture — three temperature regimes

### 1. Habitation / low-temperature loop

Serves:
- crew spaces;
- electronics that prefer mild temperatures;
- domestic water heating / recovery;
- galley;
- medbay;
- cabin / environmental loops.

Working character:
- closed nontoxic internal coolant loop;
- distributed heat exchangers / service panels;
- waste heat reused for domestic hot water and cabin heating where useful;
- remaining heat sent to body-mounted / low-temperature radiator surfaces.

This is the loop most visible in ordinary life and the source of effects such as warm floors, changed vent temperatures, and comfort-loop downshifts.

### 2. Equipment / medium-temperature loop

Serves:
- power electronics;
- cargo / mission equipment;
- pumps;
- B.O.B. compute / relay hardware;
- drive auxiliaries that do not require extreme-temperature coolant.

Working character:
- warmer external radiator surfaces than the habitation loop;
- variable heat rejection / bypass control;
- physically separate from potable water even if service access is nearby.

### 3. Drive / high-temperature loop

Serves:
- fusion-class energy source / driver;
- power conversion;
- magnetic / plasma-drive hardware;
- high-load propulsion support.

Working character:
- high-temperature coolant or heat-pipe / pumped-loop system;
- routed to compact aft radiator vanes / panels;
- significantly hotter than the habitation radiators so a much smaller area can reject the same power;
- isolated from inhabited spaces except at service interfaces.

Exact coolant remains open.

## Radiator arrangement — recommended exterior direction

Use a **hybrid body-mounted + deployable radiator system**.

### Body-mounted / structural radiators

Place ordinary low / medium thermal-rejection surfaces:
- along dorsal / lateral mid-body areas that are not routinely shadowed by other hull surfaces;
- integrated into exterior panels rather than shaped like solar arrays;
- divided into multiple independently isolated zones.

These handle:
- crew / electronics heat;
- low-power cruise;
- docked / standby thermal loads where deployment is undesirable.

### Aft high-temperature radiator vanes

For power / propulsion waste heat, use **paired radiator vanes** built into the aft shoulders / keel region.

Preferred character:
- four-ish narrow high-temperature vanes or paired banks rather than two enormous wings;
- deploy outward to create clean radiating view to space;
- fold / retract / shutter for docking, debris exposure, or rough operations;
- each bank can isolate if damaged;
- mounted aft of the main occupied domestic volume and away from the observation cupola.

They should look like thermal machinery, not decorative fins.

### Why deployable at all?

At high heat loads, radiator area quickly becomes substantial. Deployment lets the ship carry meaningful surface area without making the entire hull broad and fragile.

The Huntress can retain enough body radiator area to survive with the vanes stowed, but prolonged high-power operation should eventually require them.

This creates a useful operating constraint:
- **stowed radiators = limited thermal endurance**
- **deployed radiators = sustained power / cooling**
- **damaged radiators = the ship can still move, but the clock becomes thermal**

## Thermal buffering

The Huntress should contain **thermal accumulator / phase-change blocks** close to the high-temperature plant.

Purpose:
- absorb short high-power peaks;
- let the drive perform brief burns without instant dependence on maximum radiator deployment;
- smooth abrupt power transitions;
- store heat when debris / docking conditions require radiator shutters closed.

The stored heat must later be rejected.

This naturally supports the prose behavior in which the Huntress remains hot after demanding operations:
- deck plates stay warm;
- environmental loops are downshifted;
- ducts carry excess heat;
- comfort loads get cancelled;
- a cooling-manifold patch can buy hours rather than "fixing" the ship instantly.

This is not magic. The ship is temporarily carrying thermal debt.

## Emergency open-cycle heat dump

As a last resort, the Huntress may be able to route water or normal reaction mass through an emergency heat exchanger and vent / expel it.

Benefits:
- extremely high short-term heat rejection;
- can prevent catastrophic overheating when radiators are unavailable.

Costs:
- consumes valuable water / reaction mass;
- creates a plume and makes the ship easier to detect;
- may leave deposits / stress thermal hardware;
- not routine operation.

This should feel like throwing supplies overboard to stay alive, not a free cooling mode.

## Waste heat and drive efficiency

Do not assume all fusion / drive energy becomes heat inside the ship.

For a plausible advanced drive:
- a large fraction of energy can leave in the exhaust / directed plasma;
- some electrical / field energy may be recovered efficiently;
- only the unavoidable plant / conversion / support losses become shipboard thermal load.

This matters. If the Huntress had to radiate the full power of a high-performance interplanetary drive, radiator requirements could overwhelm a 40–45 m utility vessel.

The design therefore assumes **high drive efficiency plus high-temperature heat rejection**, not a magic heat sink.

## Radiator scaling — sanity examples only

Radiator area depends strongly on temperature.

For a high-emissivity idealized surface:
- around 350 K, one megawatt requires on the order of **1,300 m²** of radiating area;
- around 500 K, roughly **300 m² / MW**;
- around 700 K, roughly **80 m² / MW**;
- around 800 K, roughly **50 m² / MW**.

These are idealized blackbody-style scaling values, not final Huntress hardware sizing. Real geometry, view factors, sunlight, coatings, coolant approach temperature, redundancy, and shadowing increase required area.

Implication:
- low-temperature habitation heat must stay relatively low in absolute power;
- major drive waste heat should be rejected at much higher temperature;
- brief peak loads are better buffered than forcing the ship to carry enormous low-temperature radiator area.

## Refit history — recommended consequence

The modern propulsion refit should have changed **both tankage and thermal architecture**.

Possible physical archaeology:
- old large propellant saddles remain in the hull;
- modern smaller reaction-mass cells occupy only part of that volume;
- old feed trunks are capped, rerouted, or reused;
- newer high-temperature thermal trunks cross older structural logic;
- radiator-root structures / deployment mechanisms are newer than the surrounding hull;
- the walk-in engine bay contains mismatched generations of equipment;
- some strange voids / access pockets are consequences of fitting the new system into the old class.

This is a better explanation for the ship's refit texture than assigning every odd space a secret purpose.

## Exterior shape implied by propulsion / thermal systems

The current interior + engineering logic suggests:

**Forward**
- compact command / entry body.

**Mid-body**
- broader occupied / mission / domestic volume;
- body-mounted low / medium-temperature radiator skin in selected regions.

**Aft commercial body**
- main cargo volume;
- reaction-mass cells inboard / around structural margins;
- high-temperature thermal trunks running aft;
- lower-aft maintenance geography.

**Drive body**
- compact fuel magazine and power source;
- drive-support machinery;
- radiator-vane roots;
- field / nozzle structure extending aft beyond occupied rooms.

The aft hull should therefore get mechanically denser rather than simply tapering to a decorative point.
