# WILD HUNTRESS — DESIGN DECISIONS LOG

This file records Wild Huntress design decisions established during the Between Ports visual-development process.

Use the following status language:

- **LOCKED** — user-established design/canon; preserve unless explicitly revisited.
- **WORKING** — preferred direction under discussion; do not silently canonize.
- **OPEN** — unresolved.
- **TEXT EVIDENCE** — already supported by prose/Roomwright evidence; may coexist with a user decision.

Roomwright's `locked` UI/object flag is not the same thing as **LOCKED** here. Structural objects may be locked against accidental movement while still being geometric assumptions.

---

## Ship operation

### LOCKED — Solo operability

The Wild Huntress can be run by one person.

Implications for design:
- Critical flight/navigation/ship-control functions must be available to a solo operator.
- Additional bridge stations may improve workload, redundancy, specialization, or crew coordination, but must not be required for ordinary operation.
- The ship may still be designed to house and support a larger small crew.

---

## Residential / cabin area

### LOCKED — Quenby and Iri adjacency

Quenby's cabin and Iri's cabin share a wall.

Do not lose this adjacency when resolving the cabin-row layout or the existing Iri-quarters route conflict.

### LOCKED — Quenby's cabin is larger

Quenby's cabin should be one of the larger cabins in the residential area.

This does not require luxury. It can remain plain, utilitarian, and relatively small by normal-room standards while being larger than the standard crew cabins.

### WORKING — Cabin cluster

The Huntress is imagined as having five or six cabins grouped together in the same residential area, probably off the same hall/corridor.

Exact count remains OPEN.

### WORKING — Five-cabin version

If the final count is five cabins:
- two should be slightly larger cabins;
- three should be smaller standard cabins.

Arrangement/order remains OPEN.

### OPEN — Six-cabin version

If the final count is six, the size mix and exact arrangement have not yet been decided.

The existing prose names **Cabin Six**, so the numbering system must be reconciled with whichever physical cabin count is chosen.

---

## Galley

### LOCKED — Occupancy target

The galley must physically accommodate at least five people.

Seven people may fit, but seven should feel crowded / like the practical upper limit.

This supersedes any Roomwright interpretation that treats the galley as fundamentally a two-person compartment. Existing prose describing it as crowded must be reconciled through furniture arrangement, circulation, and density rather than automatically forcing a tiny room.

### WORKING — Seating

The galley is imagined with:
- a bench; and
- additional stools, chairs, or similar compact seating.

The exact seating type/count is not yet locked.

### WORKING — Galley equipment

The galley should have enough fixed or semi-fixed utility space for:
- a kettle;
- multiple cabinets/storage compartments;
- a sink;
- a food heating / hydration / synthesis appliance of some kind;
- some form of cold storage and/or hot holding.

Exact technology and arrangement remain OPEN and should be reconciled with existing prose evidence before being canonized.
