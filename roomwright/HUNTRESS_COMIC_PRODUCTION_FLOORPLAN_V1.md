# Wild Huntress — Comic Production Floor Plan V1

Status: **DRAFT — DO NOT USE AS ART-PRODUCTION FREEZE YET**

Purpose: provide a believable, repeatable spatial baseline for drawing recurring Wild Huntress backgrounds in *Between Ports*.

This is **not** a final engineering blueprint and does not canonize every centimeter. It freezes enough geometry that recurring rooms, doors, furniture, and sightlines can remain consistent from page to page.


## Diagram review correction

The first exported V1 diagrams were **not acceptable as production floor plans**. They drew each corridor / landing / junction floor as an independent rectangle, so connected circulation zones visually overlapped and internal boundary lines ran through spaces that should read as one continuous floor area. Dense labels and camera markers compounded the problem.

This is primarily a **drafting / representation error**, not evidence that every underlying room physically occupies the same volume. However, the production freeze is suspended until the plan is redrawn as actual wall / opening geometry and the few threshold-depth assumptions are normalized.

Known problem areas to resolve before re-freezing:
- main-deck corridor offset intersections;
- dry domestic-access / service-passage junction;
- Lower Operations / work-spine connection;
- residential-turn / work-spine junction;
- residential approach / dogleg / quiet-run joints;
- aft-service / aft-reconnection / freight-node convergence;
- freight-node / main-cargo doorway depth;
- small work-spine encroachments at engineering / repair recess thresholds;
- label and camera-marker collisions, especially around the wet core / garden / Cabins Five-Six and the aft freight area.

Do not use the original V1 PNG/SVG exports as visual continuity references.

## Production rule

> **Continuity determines reality. Composition determines visibility.**

An artist does not need to show every object in every panel. If a wall, hatch, table, bench, console, or corridor turn exists in V1, however, later views should respect where it is unless the layout is intentionally revised.

## Orientation

- Forward = toward bridge glass / negative Z in Roomwright.
- Aft = toward cargo / propulsion geography / positive Z.
- Port = left / negative X.
- Starboard = right / positive X.
- Main deck floor = 0 m.
- Lower deck floor = -3.0 m.

## What is frozen for comic production

### Main deck

Production-fixed relationships:
- bridge at the forward end;
- bridge hatch feeds the forward primary corridor;
- personnel / EVA airlock is close to the bridge, off the forward corridor;
- primary stair descends from the forward corridor to Lower Operations;
- legacy storage spine branches before the main corridor offset;
- primary corridor offsets before medbay, preventing direct airlock-to-medbay sightline;
- medbay opens from the aft corridor run;
- galley is a closable room at the aft domestic junction;
- separate dry domestic-service branch leads toward hygiene and the secondary ladder;
- toilet and shower are separate compartments;
- legacy storage route contains Pocket Three, Storage Four, observation dome, and deep walk-in engine bay.

Approximate art-facing dimensions:
- Bridge: 4.8 × 4.6 m, 2.25 m overhead.
- Primary corridor: ~1.1 m clear width, ~2.15 m overhead.
- Galley: ~3.4 × 4.8 m, ~2.15 m overhead.
- Medbay: ~2.6 × 2.0 m, ~2.1 m overhead.
- Storage spine: ~1.0 m clear width, ~2.05 m overhead.
- Pocket Three: ~1.25 × 1.6 m.
- Walk-in engine bay: ~2.7 × 2.4 m working footprint.
- Toilet: ~1.2 × 1.55 m.
- Shower / wash: ~1.1 × 1.75 m.

### Galley fixed background elements

These should remain spatially consistent:
- closable forward hatch;
- long counter + sink along port side;
- tin shelf above / near that work side;
- compact central prep island in the forward working half;
- heating / hydration appliance on the opposite side;
- cooling unit low in the aft half;
- real table in the aft half;
- leather-covered bench against aft wall;
- cabinet containing the bolts-in-a-mug;
- corner fan;
- movable stools / chairs may shift;
- plants are later continuity, not baseline from the beginning of the series.

The galley should read as **working half forward / eating-lingering half aft**.

### Lower deck

Production-fixed relationships:
- primary stair lands in Lower Operations, not in residential space;
- skiff / mission bay branches immediately from the landing;
- work spine is the obvious commercial continuation aft;
- residential / nav route turns away early and breaks direct sightlines to cabin doors;
- nav sits at the operational / residential seam;
- Cabins One through Four are staggered on the approach;
- wet-service dogleg breaks the route before the quiet cabin run;
- domestic stores / future garden opens from the dogleg;
- Cabin Five is Iri; Cabin Six is Quenby; they share a wall;
- passage continues beyond Cabin Six into older aft-service fabric;
- work spine passes configurable mission / flex bay, repair recess, and principal underdeck engineering access;
- aft freight / service node connects freight lock, main cargo bay, work spine, and lower-aft service route;
- main cargo bay has large freight access and a smaller service / personnel hatch;
- Nova's port-side equipment crawl sits in the lower aft spine.

Approximate art-facing dimensions:
- Lower Operations landing: ~3.0 × 2.7 m.
- Work spine: ~1.45 m clear width.
- Residential corridor: ~1.15 m clear width.
- Standard cabin: ~2.0 × 1.85 m.
- Iri / Cabin Five: ~2.1 × 2.0 m.
- Quenby / Cabin Six: ~2.4 × 2.0 m.
- Domestic stores / future garden: ~2.0 × 2.55 m.
- Mission / flex bay: ~5.2 × 6.0 m, ~2.6 m overhead.
- Main cargo / training bay: ~7.0 × 8.0 m, ~3.2 m overhead.
- Freight transfer lock: ~3.4 × 2.8 m.
- Nova equipment crawl: ~1.8 × 1.45 m, ~1.65 m overhead.

## Camera / composition anchors

These are not fixed camera lenses. They are repeatable **places to stand the imaginary camera**.

### Main deck

**M-A — Bridge doorway looking forward**
- sees pilot stations and forward glass;
- useful for ordinary watch scenes and entrances.

**M-B — Forward corridor near stair / airlock**
- establishes bridge hatch, stair, airlock, and storage-spine decision point;
- useful whenever characters split directions.

**M-C — Galley hatch looking aft**
- sees forward work half first, table / bench beyond;
- strongest default galley establishing view.

**M-D — Galley aft corner looking forward**
- sees table / bench foreground and work half / hatch behind;
- strongest relationship / conversation reverse.

**M-E — Deep storage-spine view**
- narrow older corridor with manual hardware; can reveal Pocket Three / dome access selectively.

**M-F — Engine-bay threshold**
- frame / manifold / workbench view; good for Quenby-Iri maintenance scenes.

### Lower deck

**L-A — Bottom of primary stair**
- sees Lower Operations and immediate skiff / work-route choice.

**L-B — Residential turn / nav threshold**
- sees nav and the beginning of the staggered cabin approach without exposing the whole residential area.

**L-C — Wet-core dogleg**
- useful transition view: service panel, domestic stores / future garden hatch, quiet run beyond.

**L-D — Quiet run facing Cabins Five / Six**
- recurring intimate residential background;
- door positions and shared-wall relationship must remain consistent.

**L-E — Main cargo bay personnel hatch**
- human-scale entrance into oversized empty / lightly loaded bay;
- ideal for training and mundane project scenes.

**L-F — Aft freight node**
- commercial view into main bay / freight lock / work spine.

**L-G — Nova crawl threshold**
- low cramped service-space angle; strong scale contrast with ordinary rooms.

## First background-design target

The first full visual set sheet should be the **galley**.

Required views:
1. M-C — hatch looking aft.
2. M-D — aft corner looking forward.
3. side view across the table / bench.
4. work-half close view showing sink / counter / prep island / heater-hydration side.
5. one ordinary eye-level standing view with two adults present for scale.

The galley sheet should establish:
- wall / floor material language;
- ceiling and light treatment;
- hatch shape;
- cabinetry and panel language;
- how much exposed maintenance infrastructure is visible in a lived-in room;
- fixed local color / value grouping consistent with the Between Ports visual-style lock.

Once that sheet works, it becomes the visual construction baseline for the rest of the Huntress.
