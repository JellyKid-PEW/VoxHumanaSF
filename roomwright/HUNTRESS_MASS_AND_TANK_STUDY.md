# Wild Huntress — Mass, Water, Fuel, and Tankage Study

Status: **WORKING ENGINEERING STUDY — NOT LOCKED CANON**

This study extends the current two-deck / hull-envelope work. It is meant to answer a practical question: where does the ship's mass actually live, and how can water, propulsion consumables, and refit history fit the existing interior without forcing implausible geometry?

## Design goals

The Huntress should:
- remain mechanically believable even though magic exists in the setting;
- carry enough water and consumables for long independent operation;
- use a highly closed-loop water system rather than enormous one-way life-support reserves;
- reflect a major propulsion refit that reduced propulsion-consumable volume;
- preserve distributed maintenance access throughout lived-in / working spaces;
- keep heavy fluid and machinery mass low and relatively inboard;
- avoid one giant vulnerable tank;
- allow cargo loading to change trim / center of mass in a way B.O.B. and the crew must manage.

## Water — recommended architecture

Water should probably be the Huntress's **largest ordinary fluid-storage category by volume after the refit**, but not one enormous tank.

Recommended working architecture:
- **two main clean / reclaimed-water cells** low and near the ship's longitudinal mass center;
- **one smaller domestic buffer / header reservoir** serving the galley / hygiene / habitation wet-service stack;
- **one isolated emergency reserve** that can be valved off from the ordinary loop;
- separate wastewater / brine buffers sized for reclamation-cycle operation rather than long-term storage.

The exact number of tanks remains open. The important rule is redundancy and distributed mass rather than one single cistern.

### Working scale

For a nominal six-person ship with very high reclamation efficiency, a useful visual / mass-study range is roughly **5–10 m³ total clean/reclaimed reserve**, or about **5–10 tonnes of water**.

This is deliberately generous for only six people because water also functions as:
- operational reserve for long fringe routes;
- contamination / leak margin;
- hygiene allowance;
- thermal buffer;
- useful radiation / impact-shielding mass in selected locations;
- possible emergency reaction mass if the drive accepts water.

This range is **not canon**. The final quantity should follow route endurance, propulsion behavior, and hull mass once those are better defined.

### Placement

Primary water mass should sit:
- below / near the central domestic and commercial body;
- low enough to help mass stability;
- close enough to the wet-service stack that distribution trunks are short;
- far enough inboard that a minor hull penetration does not immediately empty the ship.

Shallow or saddle-shaped cells around structural bays are preferable to one room-sized cylinder.

The already established “behind the galley tanks” sleeping location can refer to service / access space beside this domestic water / thermal buffer region without implying all ship water is directly behind the galley.

## Water reclamation

The Huntress should use a mature high-recovery loop:
- humidity condensate recovery;
- sink / shower / wash-water recovery;
- urine separation and reclamation;
- brine concentration / dewatering;
- filtration, membrane separation, catalytic / oxidative cleanup, sterilization, and continuous quality sensing;
- small solid / concentrated waste output rather than routine dumping of useful water.

The precise technology remains open.

A **~99% class** reclamation target is a useful setting direction, but should not be treated as literally perfect. The crew still loses water through maintenance, venting, contamination, EVA losses, damaged plumbing, waste handling, food, and imperfect recovery.

This gives the ship meaningful endurance without making water irrelevant.

## Domestic hot water / heat

The Huntress's domestic hot-water system should remain mechanically conventional:
- waste-heat recovery where practical;
- physical heat exchanger;
- electric / resistive or other nonmagical supplemental heating;
- local buffer / header tank;
- pumps, valves, filters, and thermal-control hardware accessible through the main and lower wet-service zones.

This is partly an operational-security choice. In the current working magic concept, ordinary magical activation is individually traceable enough that the crew avoids routine magical utility use.

Most contemporary ships may solve the same heating problem partly through magical infrastructure; the Huntress deliberately does not rely on that.

## Propulsion refit — recommended history

### Original configuration

The class originally devoted a **substantial fraction of hull volume to bulk propulsion consumables**.

The exact old fuel is open, but the original system should plausibly involve:
- large cryogenic / low-density propellant tanks, or another bulky stored working fluid;
- insulated feed trunks;
- conditioning / pumping equipment;
- tank saddles and structural members sized around those volumes.

This old architecture explains why the ship can contain oversized voids, obsolete pipe routes, contradictory service geometry, and later-converted compartments.

### Refit configuration

The Huntress later received a major propulsion modernization that separates:
1. **compact high-energy fuel / feedstock**, and
2. **reaction mass / working fluid**.

Working concept:
- the energy source uses dense manufactured cartridges, pellets, or similarly compact fusion-class feedstock;
- the modern drive achieves much higher exhaust efficiency than the factory system;
- routine reaction mass is stored in smaller dedicated tanks than the original bulk-propellant installation required;
- the drive can tolerate alternate reaction mass in emergency mode, including water, at reduced efficiency and/or increased wear.

Exact reaction chemistry, fusion species, and drive mechanism remain OPEN.

### Why characters still say “fuel”

Crew dialogue does not need to distinguish every propulsion consumable.

“Fuel” can refer colloquially to the energy-feedstock reserve or to a simplified shipboard endurance metric that combines:
- feedstock;
- reaction mass;
- drive condition;
- available power / thermal margin.

Existing prose does not need to be rewritten merely because the engineering has multiple consumables.

## Recommended present-day consumable layout

### Compact drive fuel

Place the compact fuel magazine:
- aft;
- inboard;
- structurally protected;
- close enough to the drive / injector path to avoid absurd feed runs;
- isolated from inhabited spaces and ordinary cargo impacts.

Because the material is energy-dense, it should occupy little volume compared with water and reaction mass.

### Reaction mass

Place reaction-mass tanks:
- low / inboard around the aft commercial body;
- close to propulsion / plasma-handling machinery;
- split across multiple cells for trim and redundancy;
- away from the most vulnerable exterior faces where practical.

The current study assumes reaction-mass volume is **moderate**, not dominant, because the refit greatly improved propulsion efficiency.

### Old tank volume

Do **not** turn every former tank bay into a room.

Old propulsion-storage volume can now exist as a mixture of:
- removed tanks replaced by structure / void;
- smaller modern tanks inside older tank saddles;
- converted service bays;
- widened cargo / equipment volume;
- inaccessible double-wall space;
- obsolete feed-trunk routes;
- low-observability pockets created by refit work.

This provides ship-history texture without making hidden rooms free real estate.

Pocket Two should remain unresolved and should not be explained automatically as a former fuel tank.

## Cargo and trim

Commercial cargo may shift the ship's center of mass more than ordinary water consumption does.

Therefore:
- the main cargo bay should have marked load zones / floor limits;
- B.O.B. should calculate trim and legal loading envelopes;
- some loads may require moving other cargo, shifting reaction-mass distribution, or using ballast / trim transfer;
- the crew can reject or reconfigure a contract because the load is geometrically or dynamically poor even when total mass is acceptable.

This can become visible in ordinary ship life rather than existing only in a technical appendix.

## Recommended mass order

Not exact percentages — only relative design logic:

1. **structure + propulsion / machinery**
2. **cargo when loaded**
3. **water + ordinary consumables**
4. **reaction mass**
5. **compact drive fuel**
6. **crew / furniture / small equipment**

Depending on mission and cargo state, cargo or reaction mass can move substantially in this ordering.

The important point is that compact drive fuel is *not* a giant tank anymore.

## Working placement zones in the current hull

A useful first-pass spatial arrangement is:

- **central belly under domestic / mission regions:** main water cells;
- **near the domestic wet-service stack:** smaller header / buffer / reclamation reservoirs;
- **aft-central belly around and behind the main cargo body:** reaction-mass cells;
- **deep aft / inboard near propulsion structure:** compact fuel magazine;
- **distributed service access:** pumps, manifolds, valves, filters, sensors, and isolation hardware reached from the galley / hygiene wet zone, lower wet core, work spine, aft service passage, engine bay, and deeper crawls.

This keeps heavy mass centralized while preserving the Huntress's established “crew lives inside the machine” character.

## What remains open

Do not lock yet:
- exact water tonnage;
- exact tank count;
- exact old propellant;
- exact new fusion feedstock;
- exact reaction mass;
- whether water is merely emergency-compatible or a routine secondary propellant;
- exact center of mass;
- trim-tank mechanism;
- exact radiator / heat-rejection architecture;
- exact relationship between propulsion technology and magic.

The next useful engineering pass is to place these **mass zones** inside the current hull study and check whether the resulting center-of-mass / cargo / propulsion geometry still produces a believable utility ship.
