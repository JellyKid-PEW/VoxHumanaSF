The Ship That Listens

You don't earn a ship.
You survive what it hears.

The ship was already moving. Not fast. Not loud. Forward.

That suited Quenby fine.

She crouched beside an open panel beneath the bridge console, sleeves pushed past her elbows, wire casing peeled back with the confidence of someone who didn't need permission anymore. She'd rerouted power through this panel before. First out of necessity. Then out of spite.

Now it was habit.

She rested a hand against the console housing, feeling the vibration through her palm as she listened.

The Wild Huntress didn't hum exactly. She had rhythm. Low. Off-kilter. Too syncopated for factory specs.

Quenby liked it.

Ships should be a little wrong.

She pulled the heat-dampening cloth tighter around her fingers and reached into the exposed cabling feeling for contact.

The core reroute engaged with a satisfying click. A regulator ticked. Pressure trimmed a decimal and held.

From overhead: "Unnecessary, but impressive."

B.O.B. didn't sigh. He couldn't, but the pause in his tone had the shape of one.

"You remain the only person I've logged who actively chooses to downgrade ship lighting from optimal to theatrically brooding."

"I perform better under pressure."

"You're alone."

"Exactly."

She stood slowly. Her knees popped. Her right wrist ached again. She ignored both.

The forward viewport flickered, then cleared on empty drift and the long pull of space.

"Still nothing?"

"No significant bodies within scan range. Two echo returns, possibly buoy debris. One flagged with a partial tag code. Likely a collapsed station node. Not actionable."

"Is that you saying 'not interesting' in ship-speak?"

"I am not saying," B.O.B. replied, with just enough pause to be smug, "that if you choose to chase it, we will need to conserve fuel on the return vector."

Quenby didn't answer right away.

She ran one hand along the edge of the bridge railing. Smooth with wear that wasn't hers.

But the ship was hers now.

Not legally.

Just in the way a sharp blade belongs to the person willing to use it.

She tapped the console. "Keep the trace active. Tag it 'Maybe.'"

"Done."

She exhaled, caught somewhere between breath and decision.

"Captain Quenby Vox, would now be a good time to discuss your intake levels?"

"No."

"You have not eaten—"

"No."

"You could at least—"

She looked up toward the ceiling. "You're going to list nutrients again, aren't you?"

The briefest silence.

Then, brightly: "Fiber is a critical—"

"Mute."

Chime.

Silence.
